# Core phrases

IPA notes: `ˈ` stressed syllable · `·` natural pause · General American.
Format: **"phrase" — /pronunciation/ — when to use it**.

### 1. Small talk & checking in
Greetings, reactions, and light catch-ups.

- "Where do you live? Which street do you live on? Which ward and city do you live in?" — /wer duː juː lɪv · wɪtʃ striːt duː juː lɪv ɑːn · wɪtʃ wɔːrd ənd ˈsɪti duː juː lɪv ɪn/ — asking where someone lives (VN: ward = phường, commune = xã; NZ: use "suburb")
- "How do you order from Shopee if you don't remember your own address?" — /haʊ duː juː ˈɔːrdər frʌm ˈʃɑːpiː ɪf juː doʊnt rɪˈmembər jɔːr oʊn ˈædres/ — friendly teasing (say it with a smile)
- "How long does it take you to get from home to the office? How far is your home from the office?" — /haʊ lɔːŋ dʌz ɪt teɪk juː tə ɡet frʌm hoʊm tə ði ˈɑːfɪs · haʊ fɑːr ɪz jɔːr hoʊm frʌm ði ˈɑːfɪs/ — asking about someone's commute (time + distance)
- "Our final exam is coming up. Do you know who will be testing us?" — /ˈaʊər ˈfaɪnəl ɪɡˈzæm ɪz ˈkʌmɪŋ ʌp · duː juː noʊ huː wɪl biː ˈtestɪŋ ʌs/ — asking the teacher who will run the final exam

### 6. Project & team coordination
- "I'm back in Da Nang. Thanks for the warm welcome and all the help — really enjoyed working with you all. Let's keep in touch!" — saying goodbye in a group chat after an onsite/rotation ends
- "It's been great working with you! Wishing you all the best in what's next. Let's keep in touch — take care!" — replying to a teammate's farewell
- "Sad to see you go! Thanks for all the help and the good times. All the best, and stay in touch!" — a warmer reply to a teammate's farewell
- "I'll really miss our daily lunches from when I was onsite in Hanoi — let's do it again whenever I'm back, or you make it down to Da Nang!" — a warm, personal note when a teammate leaves
- "Who made sync-skills?" — /huː meɪd sɪŋk skɪlz/ — asking who built a tool, script, or doc (swap in the name)

### 7. Hosting a visitor
Welcoming a guest (e.g. a senior client) and taking them out.

- "How was your flight here?" — /haʊ wʌz jɔːr flaɪt hɪr/ — greeting a visitor who flew in (e.g. a senior client guest)
- "Was the traffic okay on the way from the airport?" — /wʌz ðə ˈtræfɪk oʊˈkeɪ ɑːn ðə weɪ frʌm ði ˈerpɔːrt/ — follow-up with a visitor
- "What do you think of the traffic in Vietnam?" — /wʌt duː juː θɪŋk əv ðə ˈtræfɪk ɪn ˌviːetˈnɑːm/ — light topic with a visitor new to Vietnam
- "I would love to take you out for some local food. Is there any food you cannot eat?" — /aɪ wʊd lʌv tə teɪk juː aʊt fɔːr sʌm ˈloʊkəl fuːd · ɪz ðer ˈeni fuːd juː ˈkænɑːt iːt/ — inviting them out, then checking what they can eat (say both together)
- "I heard CEOs are always busy, meeting people and travelling all the time. Do you really spend more nights in hotels than at home?" — /aɪ hɜːrd ˌsiː iː ˈoʊz ɑːr ˈɔːlweɪz ˈbɪzi · ˈmiːtɪŋ ˈpiːpəl ənd ˈtrævəlɪŋ ɔːl ðə taɪm · duː juː ˈrɪli spend mɔːr naɪts ɪn hoʊˈtelz ðən æt hoʊm/ — friendly curiosity with a senior guest (say it with a smile)
- "What do you like to do when you are not working?" — /wʌt duː juː laɪk tə duː wen juː ɑːr nɑːt ˈwɜːrkɪŋ/ — light topic about life outside work
- "Where are you staying? How are you getting to the office? Did you rent a motorbike to try the Da Nang traffic? I guess you already have plans for every evening, right? Partying all night on the tourist street, like An Thuong?" — /wer ɑːr juː ˈsteɪɪŋ · haʊ ɑːr juː ˈɡetɪŋ tə ði ˈɑːfɪs · dɪd juː rent ə ˈmoʊtərbaɪk tə traɪ ðə dɑː nɑːŋ ˈtræfɪk · aɪ ɡes juː ɔːlˈredi hæv plænz fɔːr ˈevri ˈiːvnɪŋ raɪt · ˈpɑːrtiɪŋ ɔːl naɪt ɑːn ðə ˈtʊrɪst striːt laɪk ɑːn tuːɑːŋ/ — chatting with a colleague on a work trip to Da Nang (pause after each question; party joke for colleagues only, not clients)
- Guest: "Is there anything you would like me to bring from New Zealand?" → Me: "Chocolate is my favorite, so I would love to try some NZ chocolate." — /ˈtʃɑːklət ɪz maɪ ˈfeɪvərɪt · soʊ aɪ wʊd lʌv tə traɪ sʌm en ziː ˈtʃɑːklət/ — when a guest offers to bring something from home
- Guest: "Have you been to Ho Chi Minh City?" (or "Saigon") → Me: "Yes, I worked there for a while, and I have been back a few times for work. It is too crowded and polluted for me, so I still prefer living in Da Nang." — /jes · aɪ wɜːrkt ðer fɔːr ə waɪl · ənd aɪ hæv bɪn bæk ə fjuː taɪmz fɔːr wɜːrk · ɪt ɪz tuː ˈkraʊdɪd ənd pəˈluːtɪd fɔːr miː · soʊ aɪ stɪl prɪˈfɜːr ˈlɪvɪŋ ɪn dɑː nɑːŋ/ — when asked about HCMC ("for me" keeps it personal, not a complaint)
