# Core phrases

### 1. Small talk & checking in

- "Our final exam is coming up. Do you know who will be testing us?"
- "Did you enjoy playing badminton with us last time? Did you win any games?"
- "Did you enjoy the team lunch last time? Did you see what we're really like outside the classroom?"
- "Sometimes I notice I'm speaking English with Vietnamese grammar. Do you hear that a lot?"
- "It's not common in Vietnam, so I'll only use it with foreigners."
- "Right now I'm working toward a solution architect or project manager role, because I think coding alone is becoming less valuable, while communication and seeing the big picture matter more."
- "After a couple of months of working with AI, I realized that English is very important these days, especially spoken English."
- "Don't worry too much about the future. Just stay healthy and happy, and you'll be ready to face any challenge. And good food and drinks always help!"
- Them: "How far is your home from the office?" → Me: "I live about fifteen kilometres from the office."
- "I live on the other side of the city, in the old part, so I have to go through the city centre. It's really crowded there. This side is newer, with wider roads and fewer people."
- "I try to get my work done at the office, because when I get home, my kids won't let me touch my laptop. They just want me to play with them all the time!"
- "You should see Mr T's house. It's beautiful, and the home office looks really professional!"
- "When I drink a lot, my wife drives me home. Driving after drinking is dangerous, and the fines in Vietnam are really high."
- "I drive a small VinFast electric car. I bought it recently, after the taxes and fees for electric cars went down, and the government also gives some support. Charging is about three to four times cheaper than petrol in Vietnam. It's fast too: thirty minutes of charging gives me about a hundred kilometres, and a full charge takes about an hour and lasts almost two hundred kilometres."

### 6. Project & team coordination
- "Could I ask for your honest feedback on my English? Also, is the team's English clear enough for you in meetings, or is there anything we should improve?"
- Them: "You're on the right track." → Me: "Thanks! I'm glad to hear that."
- "Could you talk to the team in the next meeting about why clear English communication is important? I think it will have more impact if it comes from you."
- "It will take time for the team to adapt to the new way of working, like doing less manual code review and focusing more on design and solution review."
- "We might be busy with the client this afternoon, so it's safer to move it. I'll check our schedule with them and let you know a new time soon."
- "I'm ready for my 1-1 review today or tomorrow. It only takes a few minutes, so feel free to message me any time :)" → Them: "Can you do 1pm?" → Me: "Yes, it works for me. See you at 1pm!"

### 7. Hosting a visitor

- "When are you arriving in Da Nang, and when are you planning to come to the office?"
- "I guess your evenings will be busy with our managers, but do you have a free evening? I'd love to take you out for some local food and drinks."
- "Great! Let's finalise on Monday. You have a local guide this time, so get ready for things no big tour can offer!"
- "No problem! So it'll be just our team tonight, and we'll plan another evening with you."
- "Are you going back to the hotel first, or straight to the restaurant tonight?" → Me: "I'll go straight to the restaurant after work."
- "Let's eat a big lunch today, so we can skip dinner and save room for more beer at the party tonight!"
- "How many people are coming to the party tonight?"
- "Your hotel is on my way home, so I can drop you off."
- "You look younger in person than on camera!"
- "You look just like your photos!"
- "How was your flight here?"
- "I heard your flight was delayed. I hope you're not too tired! Are you still planning to come to the office today, or would you like to rest first?"
- Guest: "I'll be in the office in another 45 mins - 1 hour, just catching up on some work." → Me: "No problem, take your time. See you soon!"
- "We've booked a meeting room for you for the whole week, so you can use it any time without worrying about someone else taking it."
- "During lunch, you can take a nap in your chair, have a coffee at the café on the ground floor, or go up to the rooftop on the 6th floor to relax, whatever you prefer."
- "Was the traffic okay on the way from the airport?"
- "What do you think of the traffic in Vietnam?"
- "I'd love to take you out for some local food. Is there any food you can't eat?"
- "This sauce is made from fermented shrimp, and it has a very strong smell. Would you like to try a little, or would you prefer fish sauce?"
- "I heard CEOs are always busy, meeting people and travelling all the time. Do you really spend more nights in hotels than at home?" → Follow-up: "Have you ever met anyone famous on your trips?"
- "What do you like to do when you're not working?"
- "What do you usually do at lunchtime?" → Me: "In Vietnam, we usually get a one-hour lunch break, and some companies even give an hour and a half." → Follow-up: "So what time do people usually finish work in New Zealand?"
- "Where are you staying? How are you getting to the office? Did you rent a motorbike to try the Da Nang traffic? I guess you already have plans for every evening, right? Partying all night on the tourist street, like An Thuong?"
- Guest: "Is there anything you'd like me to bring from New Zealand?" → Me: "Chocolate is my favorite, so I'd love to try some from NZ."
- Guest: "Have you been to Ho Chi Minh City?" (or "Saigon") → Me: "Yes, I worked there for a while, and I've been back a few times since. It's too crowded and polluted for me, so I still prefer living in Da Nang."
- "Have you tried the local beer here? How is it different from beer in New Zealand?"
- "For me, local places are more fun than the big tourist ones. Would you like me to show you some?"
- "In Vietnam, there are small local restaurants on both sides of the street, so it's really easy to find something to eat. When we go out, we usually try two or three places, and we often finish with chè, a sweet Vietnamese dessert."
- Guest: "Do you come here often?" → Me: "I studied at university here and then stayed to work, so I know the old streets quite well. But now I live and work in the newer part of the city, so I don't come to the old centre much anymore."
- "You sing so well! You should visit us more often."
