# Grammar notes

## other vs others

- **other** = adjective, always followed by a noun → "other people", "the other way"
- **others** = pronoun, stands alone → "Some agree, others don't."
- Singular → **another**

Rule: noun after it → *other*. Nothing after it → *others*.

## 's — /s/ or /z/?

The sound **before** it decides.

- After voiceless p / t / k / f / th → **/s/** → that's, it's, what's
- After vowels and voiced consonants → **/z/** → how's, where's, he's, there's

Test: put a hand on your throat. Vibration before the 's' → /z/.

## Uncountable nouns take a singular verb

No plural form → always singular verb (is / looks / has).

- *The code **looks** good* ✅ (not "look")
- *This information **is** helpful* ✅
- You can't say "two codes" / "two informations".
- "everything else" is also singular → *everything else **looks** good*.

## Note-style vs spoken shortening

- **Note-style (writing only):** drop is/are/were/the → "Comments addressed." "Build passing." "Ready for review." Good for PR comments, commits, chat status.
- **Spoken shortening:** drop the subject at the front, keep the verb → "Sounds good." "Got it." "Makes sense." Never drop the verb when speaking.
- In a meeting, put the words back: not "Comments addressed" but "The comments have been addressed."

## go / come / arrive / get

- **go** = away from where the speaker is → "I'm going to the office."
- **come** = toward the speaker *or the listener* → "Come here!" / "I'll come to your place." (not "go")
- **arrive** = the moment you reach a place, a bit formal. **at** a small place, **in** a city/country, never **to** → "I arrived at the airport." / "She arrived in Da Nang."
- **get** = same as arrive, but natural in speech. Uses **to** → "What time did you get to work?" No preposition with home / here / there → "I got home late."

## in / on / at (big → small)

| | **in** (big, general) | **on** (middle) | **at** (small, exact) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time | in November, in 2026, in the morning | on Friday, on 20 November | at 1pm, at lunchtime, at night |
| Place | in Da Nang, in Vietnam, in the room | on the street, on the 6th floor | at the office, at the airport, at home |

**No in/on/at** before **next / last / this / every / the next** → next week, last Friday, this morning, every Monday, the next day
- "**on** Friday" but "**last** Friday" · "**in** the morning" but "**this** morning"
- Day + part of day takes **on** → "**on** Monday morning"
- "on the next day" isn't wrong, just formal (stories, reports)

## go for + activity

- **go for** + noun = go out to do something → go for lunch / a coffee / a drink / a walk / a run
- **go to** + place → go **to** the café, but go **for** a coffee

## both / all go before "the"

- ✅ **both sides** of the street (most common) · ✅ both the sides (rare) · ❌ the both sides
- Same with **all** → "all the people", not "the all people"

## Who as subject vs object

*Ryan played badminton with Viktor last night.*

- Ask about **Viktor** (object) → use did: "Who **did** Ryan play with?"
- Ask about **Ryan** (subject) → no did, verb keeps its tense: "Who **played** badminton with Viktor?"
- Same rule as "Who approves the refund?" (no do/does/did when who is the subject)

## -ed endings: /t/, /d/ or /ɪd/?

The sound **before** -ed decides (same idea as 's).

- After voiceless p / k / f / s / sh / ch / x → **/t/** → finished, fixed, worked, asked
- After vowels and voiced b / g / l / m / n / v / z → **/d/** → played, called, planned
- After t / d → **/ɪd/** (extra syllable) → wanted, needed, completed
