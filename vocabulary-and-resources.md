# Vocabulary & resources

## Vocabulary picked up

| Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| when you get a chance | when you have time, no rush |
| a win | a small success — "that's a win" |
| sleep in | wake up late on purpose *(≠ sleep late = go to bed late)* |
| blunt | direct in a harsh way |
| tooling cost | the time and effort of setting up and maintaining a tool |
| recover | get your energy back *(≠ recall)* |
| tiring day | a day that makes you tired *(≠ tired day)* |

## Sounding warm with humans (not just correct)

Bare commands can feel cold or bossy to a person, even when the English is correct. Soften with these and even a short request sounds friendly:

- **"Could you...?"** instead of a plain command
- **"thanks"** / **"no worries"**
- "Nice work!" instead of just "good job"

| To a tool (fine) | To a human (warmer) |
|---|---|
| do this | Could you do this? |
| don't check that | No need to check that, thanks. |
| good job | Nice work! Thanks. |

## Learning resources

**YouTube search terms that work:** `business English meeting phrases` · `English for international teams` · `daily standup English scrum` · `polite English at work non-native`

Golden keywords: **"business English"** and **"for non-native"**. Avoid the word "native" — it only surfaces idioms.

Channels: Business English Pod · Speak Confident English · BBC Learning English "English at Work"

**Using my own meetings as a source:** listen for the *situation*, not the wording — colleagues often speak incorrectly. Note the situation, then ask for the correct phrasing.

**Chat workflow:** write team-chat messages as close to spoken English as possible, so chat and speech reinforce each other. Then **read good sentences aloud three times before sending** — that closes the gap between knowing a phrase (10 seconds to think in chat) and producing it (half a second in speech).
