# English Speaking — Phrase Bank & Practice System

Ryan · Vietnamese software developer · working on an international team
Core principle: **say less, but say it well.**

IPA notes: `ˈ` marks the stressed syllable · `·` marks a natural pause · General American accent.

---

## How to coach me (rules for a new session)

- Keep replies short, simple, spoken English. Slow pace, not necessarily fewer words.
- Treat every message of mine as if I said it out loud.
- End every reply with one line: *Say it like this: "[natural spoken version]"*
- Flag wrong grammar, unnatural phrasing, and wrong ending sounds. **Skip small slips.**
- **Three-tries rule:** try a sound correction about three times, then park it and move on.
- Clarity beats sounding native. Half my meetings are with non-native speakers, so prefer full forms over contractions.
- Give me **one** go-to phrase per situation, never a list of options. Hesitation kills reflex.
- Don't read long lists aloud in voice mode. I'll read them in chat.
- Explain in Vietnamese using tôi/bạn.

### The three-tier register test

| Tier | Example | What I do with it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — native idiom / slang | "circle back", "call it a day" | understand it, don't say it |
| 2 — natural but transparent | "Nothing from me", "Let me get back to you on that" | **my target zone** |
| 3 — textbook / over-formal | "I would like to inquire whether..." | avoid |

Test: if an Indian or Japanese colleague would have to guess the meaning, cut it.

## Phrase bank

### 1. Opening the daily
Break the silence while people join.

- "How is your morning going?" — /haʊ ɪz jɔːr ˈmɔːrnɪŋ ˈɡoʊɪŋ/
- "Anything fun this weekend?" — /ˈeniθɪŋ fʌn ðɪs ˈwiːkend/
- "How is the weather over there?" — /haʊ ɪz ðə ˈweðər ˈoʊvər ðer/

> Use the full **"How is"**, not "How's". It avoids the /z/ and still sounds natural.

### 2. Moving through the board
Ask for an update, then move on.

- "How is your story going?" — /haʊ ɪz jɔːr ˈstɔːri ˈɡoʊɪŋ/
- "Next up, Ryan's story. How is it going?" — /nekst ʌp ˈraɪənz ˈstɔːri · haʊ ɪz ɪt ˈɡoʊɪŋ/

### 3. After someone finishes
Acknowledge, no dead air.

- "Thanks, Ryan. Next up..." — /θæŋks ˈraɪən · nekst ʌp/

### 4. Closing the meeting
Signal the end clearly.

- "Alright, that's all the stories for this daily meeting." — /ɔːlˈraɪt ðæts ɔːl ðə ˈstɔːriz fɔːr ðɪs ˈdeɪli ˈmiːtɪŋ/
- "Thanks everyone — have a good day." — /θæŋks ˈevriwʌn · hæv ə ɡʊd deɪ/

### 5. Finishing my own update
- "That's it from me." — /ðæts ɪt frʌm miː/

### 6. No questions
- "Nothing from me." — /ˈnʌθɪŋ frʌm miː/

### 7. Need thinking time
- "Let me get back to you on that." — /let mi ɡet bæk tə juː ɑːn ðæt/

### 8. Didn't catch something
- "Sorry, can you repeat that?" — /ˈsɑːri · kən juː rɪˈpiːt ðæt/
- If it's the connection: "You cut out for a second." — /juː kʌt aʊt fɔːr ə ˈsekənd/

### 9. Asking for clarification
- "What do you mean by that?" — /wʌt duː juː miːn baɪ ðæt/
- Or name the thing: "What do you mean by *staging*?"

### 10. Asking about scale
- "How big is it?" — /haʊ bɪɡ ɪz ɪt/

### 11. PR reviews
- Asking: "Can you review my PR when you get a chance?" — /kən juː rɪˈvjuː maɪ piː ˈɑːr wen juː ɡet ə tʃæns/
- Approving: "Looks good to me." — /lʊks ɡʊd tə miː/
- With feedback: "I left a few comments." — /aɪ left ə fjuː ˈkɑːments/
- Approving after minor notes: "Everything else looks good." — /ˈevriθɪŋ els lʊks ɡʊd/

> "Everything else" is singular → always **looks**, not "look". Clearer than "otherwise" or "the rest" for a non-native reader.

### 12. Asking project status
- "Where are we on the August release?" — /wer ɑːr wi ɑːn ði ˈɔːɡəst rɪˈliːs/

> Always name the release. "Milestone" alone is too vague.

### 13. Disagreeing politely
- "I get that, but I think we should wait." — /aɪ ɡet ðæt · bʌt aɪ θɪŋk wi ʃʊd weɪt/

### 14. Opening a one-to-one
- "How is the team doing?" — /haʊ ɪz ðə tiːm ˈduːɪŋ/

### 15. Steering the session
- "Let's move on." — /lets muːv ɑːn/

### 16. Asking someone to help fix my English
- "Could you flag anything that doesn't sound natural?" — /kʊd juː flæɡ ˈeniθɪŋ ðæt ˈdʌzənt saʊnd ˈnætʃrəl/
- Longer: "I want to get better at speaking by chatting with you like I would with a person. Could you flag anything that doesn't sound natural, like you did above?"

> "doesn't sound natural" beats the idiom "sounds off" — Tier 2, clear to any non-native.

## Mouth muscle exercises

Vietnamese uses a narrower jaw range and clips syllables. English needs more tongue travel. These build the range.

### Exercise 1 — Pen holding *(start here)*

1. Put a pen sideways between your teeth, biting lightly.
2. Read aloud for 2 minutes.
3. Take the pen out and read the same lines again.

**Why it works:** the pen blocks your jaw, so your tongue and lips must do all the work. When you remove it, the movement feels easy and your articulation is sharper.

**Lines to read with the pen in** — use phrases you already know, so your brain isn't busy with new words:
- "Thanks, Ryan. Next up."
- "Can you review my PR when you get a chance?"
- "Where are we on the August release?"
- "Alright, that's all the stories for this daily meeting."

### Exercise 2 — Vowel stretching

Hold each sound for **2 seconds**. Ten rounds. Keep this order.

| Sound | IPA | Mouth position |
|---|---|---|
| ah | /ɑː/ | jaw wide open |
| ee | /iː/ | lips wide, like a smile |
| oo | /uː/ | lips tight circle |
| ay | /eɪ/ | mid, then glide up |
| oh | /oʊ/ | mid, lips round |

These five are the extreme corner positions. Every other English vowel sits inside that range.

### Exercise 3 — Tongue twisters

Short ones only. Slow first, speed later.

- red lorry, yellow lorry
- she sells seashells
- big black bug bit
- three free throws
- toy boat, toy boat

## Pronunciation watchlist

| Word | Comes out as | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| beach | beat | add /tʃ/ — "bee...ch" |
| boring | pouring | "BOR...ring" ✅ fixed |
| sprint | spread / spring | finish the /nt/ ✅ fixed |
| daily | daddy | ✅ fixed |
| basis | by six | "BAY...sis", no K |
| it vs eat | eat | short, lazy /ɪ/ ✅ both fixed |
| how's | house | ends with a buzz /z/ — **parked**, use "how is" |
| your side | outside | use "over there" instead |
| fun | from | say "sun", swap S for F |
| next up | next app | "up" like "cup" — **parked** |
| going | growing | no R |
| could | court | rhymes with "wood" — or just drop it |
| next | neck / met | light /t/ tap ✅ fixed |
| world | word | say "were" + L ✅ fixed |
| watch | what | needs the /tʃ/ |
| baked | bake | ends /t/ — "baykt" |
| system | — | ✅ fixed |
| respond | respot | ends /d/, stress on "POND" ✅ fixed |
| thanks | hence / tenth | TH onset — **parked** |
| should | soot / source | **parked** |
| polluted | bloated | **parked** |

**Core diagnosis:** I know the sounds. My mouth moves on before the ending lands. It's a speed problem, not a knowledge problem. **Slowing down about 10% fixes most of it.** Accuracy collapses the moment I speed up — so lock accuracy first, speed later.

Speaking slowly is not unprofessional. Slow and clear sounds thoughtful; fast and unclear sounds nervous.

### IPA symbols worth knowing (only 5)

| Symbol | Example | Note |
|---|---|---|
| /ɪ/ vs /iː/ | sit vs seat | short lazy vs long wide |
| /tʃ/ | bea**ch** | tongue back, air out |
| /z/ | how'**s** | buzz, not hiss |
| /t/ /d/ | nex**t**, respon**d** | my vanishing endings |
| /ɜː/ | w**or**ld | say "were" first |

### p vs b — the hand test

Hand in front of your mouth. **P**ig = puff of air. **B**ig = no puff.
Drill: pig/big, pond/bond, pack/back.

### Daily drill (2 min)

- /z/ — how's, was, is, does, goes
- /tʃ/ — beach, teach, watch, catch, much
- final /t/ — sprint, best, want, next, it
- short /ɪ/ — sit, it, big, this, still

## Grammar notes

### other vs others

- **other** = adjective, always followed by a noun → "other people", "the other way"
- **others** = pronoun, stands alone → "Some agree, others don't."
- Singular → **another**

Rule: noun after it → *other*. Nothing after it → *others*.

### 's — /s/ or /z/?

The sound **before** it decides.

- After voiceless p / t / k / f / th → **/s/** → that's, it's, what's
- After vowels and voiced consonants → **/z/** → how's, where's, he's, there's

Test: put a hand on your throat. Vibration before the 's' → /z/.

## Learning resources

**YouTube search terms that work:** `business English meeting phrases` · `English for international teams` · `daily standup English scrum` · `polite English at work non-native`

Golden keywords: **"business English"** and **"for non-native"**. Avoid the word "native" — it only surfaces idioms.

Channels: Business English Pod · Speak Confident English · BBC Learning English "English at Work"

**Using my own meetings as a source:** listen for the *situation*, not the wording — colleagues often speak incorrectly. Note the situation, then ask for the correct phrasing.

**Chat workflow:** write team-chat messages as close to spoken English as possible, so chat and speech reinforce each other. Then **read good sentences aloud three times before sending** — that closes the gap between knowing a phrase (10 seconds to think in chat) and producing it (half a second in speech).
